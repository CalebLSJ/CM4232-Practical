## Objective

In this experiment, the rate coefficient for the hydrolysis of *tert*-butyl chloride in a water/acetone mixture will be measured.

\[
(CH_3)_3C{-}Cl + H_2O \rightarrow (CH_3)_3C{-}OH + HCl
\]

The solvent in which a reaction is carried out often influences the reaction rate. In this experiment, you will explore the substitution reaction between water and tert-butyl chloride (2-chloro-2-methylpropane) in water/acetone.

---

## Materials

### Chemicals
- 150 mL 0.01 M NaOH  
- 1 mL tert-butyl chloride (distributed with 1 mL automatic pipette)  
- 155 mL acetone  
- 100 mL DI water  
- 1 mL bromothymol blue  

### Apparatus
- 1 × 50.00 mL burette  
- 2.00 mL pipette

- 4 × 100 cm³ conical flasks

- 100 mL bottle with cap

## Procedure

1. Place 150 cm³ of 0.0100 M NaOH (aq) in a 200 mL beaker.  

2. Prepare 100 cm³ of a 50/50 acetone/DI water mixture (by volume) in a 200 mL bottle with a cap and a large magnetic bar. Mix well and stir continuously.  

3. Add 1.00 cm³ (± 0.05 cm³) of tert-butyl chloride (using the 2 mL pipette) to the mixture.  

   - Immediately cap the bottle, shake well, and begin stirring.  
   - Start the stopwatch at once.  
   - Keep the bottle capped except when removing aliquots.  

4. Prepare an **“infinity time” sample**:  
   - Add 5.00 cm³ of reaction mixture to a 100 mL bottle containing 50 cm³ of water.  
   - Cap and stir thoroughly.  
   - The high water concentration drives the reaction to completion.  

5. At specified time intervals, remove 2 cm³ aliquots of the reaction mixture and:  
   - Place into a 100 mL conical flask on ice containing 15 cm³ pre-chilled acetone.  
   - The acetone effectively quenches (or slows) the reaction.  
   - Add 3 drops of bromothymol blue indicator.  
   - Titrate **as quickly as possible** with standardized NaOH to a blue endpoint lasting 10 seconds.  
   - Record the volume used.  

6. After all data collection, titrate the **infinity time sample** in the same manner.

## Treatment of Results

1. Calculate \([HCl]_t\) (mol dm⁻³) at each sampling time. Show a sample calculation.  

2. From the infinity time data, calculate \([HCl]_{\infty}\) (mol dm⁻³).  

3. Plot:

\[
\ln([HCl]_{\infty} - [HCl]_t) \ \text{vs} \ t
\]

- Draw the best-fit straight line.  
- The gradient gives the **pseudo-first-order rate coefficient**.  
- Using Excel is recommended.  

4. Derive why plotting  
\[
\ln([HCl]_{\infty} - [HCl]_t) \text{ vs } t
\]  
is necessary to determine the reaction rate.  

5. Propose a mechanism for the hydrolysis of tert-butyl chloride in a polar solvent.  

6. Discuss possible experimental errors and suggest improvements.
